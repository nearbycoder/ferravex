## Concrete geometry and failure behavior

- Planar world `(x,y)` in caller world units. A 3D XZ adapter is explicitly `vec2(position.x, position.z)` at the call site; no 3D plane is inferred. `WorldYAxis::Up` or `Down` chooses positive world Y's initial screen direction. Positive `rotation_radians` rotates clockwise in screen coordinates after that convention; map X points right and Y down. Any finite f32 angle is accepted; large angles can lose phase precision.
- Fixed policy: preserve the world center at the map center, uniformly aspect-fit the complete rotated world rectangle, center the remaining letterbox space, and never crop/stretch. `footprint()` returns the rotated world boundary in world corner order min/min, max/min, max/max, min/max. Its axis-aligned panel is not a substitute for this footprint when painting or interpreting map content.
- World bounds are closed for projection. `Inside` means inside those world bounds, regardless of screen rotation. Outside points return `Outside` under `Preserve` or `Clamped` under `ClampToEdge`. Both include the same `OffMapIndicator`: the center-to-point ray intersection with the rotated world boundary and a normalized screen direction. This is a ray intersection, not independently clamped axes or a nearest point. A point outside the world can remain inside the panel's bounding box under rotation; its outside status and indicator are retained.
- `ClampToEdge` always returns positions in the panel's closed bounds for accepted finite points. `Preserve` explicitly permits a finite position outside the panel, and rejects a result that overflows f32. Both policies reject nonfinite supplied points. No missing/unknown markers are generated.
- `pick()` consumes the same logical coordinates as `config.map`. Both the map and caller `clip` use existing `Rect::contains` half-open edges; right/bottom edges remain excluded, even though projection of a world boundary is allowed there. Empty valid clips are inert. Picking rejects letterbox space and rotated corners outside the world footprint. Nonfinite pointers and invalid clip rectangles are errors rather than misses.
- Rectangle endpoints are the existing f32 `min` and `min + size`, matching UI rectangle/pointer geometry; differences are widened only after those endpoints are established. Negative/zero/subnormal extents, nonfinite/overflowing edges and collapsed f32 edges are rejected. Fitted rounded corners must form a strictly convex quadrilateral: adjacent collapse, collinearity or reversed turns return `Unrepresentable`, including when a large panel origin erases the fit's height despite distinct adjacent vertices.
- Rotation, differences, scale, ray parameters, directions and inverse arithmetic use f64. Outputs round to existing f32 vectors. Pick inverse permits half the larger adjacent f32 spacing per screen coordinate, transformed into world axes, plus `16 * f64::EPSILON * world_half_extent` arithmetic tolerance, then clamps that fringe to closed world edges. This keeps rounded displayed vertices pickable without expanding half-open panel/clip hit bounds. The fit is analytical before final rounding; extremely coarse coordinates/thin fits remain approximate, not exact predicates.
- Failed `reconfigure()` leaves the last-good transform unchanged; successful replacement is the scene/resize reset operation. There are no remembered points, journal history or input edges to clear. Scalar queries cannot affect later queries.
- `project_into()` checks input count and output capacity first, then validates every projection before writing. Rejected invalid/overflowing/capacity batches leave the **entire** output unchanged. Successful batches preserve input order, write only `output[..returned_count]` and preserve the tail. Empty input returns zero. The 4,096-point cap bounds work to at most 8,192 scalar projections via two passes, with constant scratch space and no allocated container. Caller must draw only the returned prefix; stale capacity slots are not markers.
