# Siege Foundry

A first-party Ferravex example with a shared WebGL2 browser host and native renderer entry point. See [platform support and verification](../../docs/PLATFORMS.md) for the OS libraries and device checks that apply.

A low-poly 3D demolition range built with Ferravex's `GameSession`, original rotational 3D physics, cached meshes, and built-in lit material. Bring down all six rust-red blocks with eight iron cannonballs. Supporting sandstone blocks are physical bodies too: removing a low support can collapse an entire tower. Foreman Gus stands well clear of the line of fire and has opinions about everything you do.

## What makes it fun

- **Demolition score.** Each target pays 100, multiplied by its place in a chain when one shot fells several (×2, ×3 … capped at ×5); each sandstone support pays 25. Bonuses: **DOMINO EFFECT!** (one shot topples targets in two towers, +250), **ONE-SHOT WONDER!** (four targets from one shot, +500), **OVERKILL!** (kicking the supports out from under a tower that already lost both targets, +150) and **SPARE SHELLS** (+200 for every unused cannonball at a win). The top bar's score rolls up as points land.
- **Stars and records.** Clear the range in three shots for three stars, five for two, any win for one. The top bar shows the stars still on offer. Your best score and best star rating are saved locally (autoplay and smoke runs never write records).
- **The wobble… TIMBER! beat.** When a tower's top target starts to lean, a "WOBBLE..." tag appears and Gus frets; when its last target falls the banner shouts **TIMBER!** with the volley's points. A ball that seems to miss but triggers a slow topple later earns **WAIT... IT COUNTS!** and the miss is struck from the record.
- **Foreman Gus.** A big lad in a hi-vis vest and hard hat beside the right tower. He greets each run, frets during wobbles, throws his arms up at collapses, heckles misses (gently), nudges you toward the supports after repeated misses, counts down the last shell and remarks on bonuses. Lines rotate per category and never repeat back to back, including across retries.
- **Juice.** Chunky rust/sand/soot debris, impact dust, muzzle flash and smoke, a smoke trail behind the newest ball, expanding impact rings, rising score pops, a camera kick on each shot plus decaying shake on impacts, a squash of the cannon carriage, and Gus's hops.
- **A secret.** Something hangs above the towers. Ring it for a bonus and a confused foreman.
- **Results card.** Stars, the final score, NEW PERSONAL BEST / your best, shots, best chain and misses, a silly rank title with a quip (from CONSCIENTIOUS OBJECTOR to WRECKING BALL WIZARD), and a next-goal hook (the next star's shot target, the secret, or the score to beat). The card appears after a short beat so the final collapse and spare-shell bonus land first.

```sh
cargo run --release -p siege-foundry
cargo test -p siege-foundry --offline --locked -j 2
cargo run --release -p siege-foundry -- --autoplay --smoke-frames 480 --screenshot siege-foundry.png
```

- **Enter**, **Space**, or **Start**: enter the range. Starting does not spend a shot.
- **Left / Right** or **A / D**: change cannon heading.
- **Up / Down** or **W / S**: raise / lower the barrel.
- **Space** or **Fire**: shoot. The dotted line previews the gravity-driven arc.
- **Drag in the scene**: aim with a mouse or touch; use the Fire button to shoot.
- **Escape** or **Pause / Resume**: freeze / resume the range.
- **R** or **Rebuild**: restore all bodies, eight shots, the original aim, timers and feedback.
- After a result, **Enter**, **Space**, or **Again / Retry** rebuilds without firing. The next fresh Fire press launches a shot.

Debug builds (never release or Wasm) accept visual-review hooks for screenshots: `SIEGE_WINDOW=390x700`, `SIEGE_PAUSE_AT=N`, `SIEGE_PLAN=bell,miss,...` (scripted autoplay aims) and `SIEGE_FAKE_BEST=POINTS`.

The top bar shows six target pips (rust until knocked down, then a green tick) and the score with the stars still on offer. The bottom panel shows eight shell markers with a count, heading (**YAW**) and elevation (**ELEV**) in degrees when there is room, one status/coaching line, and the Fire, Rebuild and Pause buttons. Launch power is fixed at 28 m/s; adjust the angle to change the trajectory. The dotted arc ends in a reticle coloured by what the ball will hit first; until something has fallen it is also labelled **TARGET** or **SUPPORT**. Fire shows **Loading** and a progress bar during its 0.8-second reload. A press during reload is discarded, so it cannot launch an unexpected shot later. **Shot away** confirms an accepted shot; target knockdowns show a count and turn muted green. Onboarding hints (how to aim and fire, then a tip about low supports after a miss) fade once learned for the session. Ready, Paused and results cards always name the next action. One banner slot and one speech bubble keep messages from stacking; on narrow screens Gus's bubble moves below the towers with a hard-hat speaker tag, banners shrink, and cards drop their least important lines instead of covering the buttons. Shot, impact, support, target, wobble, bonus, bell and result cues use the shared audio system; enable browser sound with the Sound button.

The keys above are defaults. **Controls** supports remapping; the Fire and start/resume/retry hints read the current `PlayerInput` bindings after sampling, including **Unbound** actions. Touch hints point to the visible buttons rather than unused preset touch channels. Long binding lists are shortened with an ellipsis at a fixed font size; open Controls for the full mapping. Narrow layouts retain separate aim, power, ammo and status rows and 44-pixel-high buttons. Starting, retrying or rebuilding still spends no ammunition.

The view keeps the cannon, standing towers and low support arcs between the HUD panels on narrow screens. Framing uses the same fixed perspective and eye, fitting only its screen projection; it does not move with aim or debris, alter fog/depth, or change input sensitivity or ballistic rules. High arcs and flying debris can still leave the framed range. The header uses a complete short objective at 320 pixels.

**Details → Player tools → Game and player UI motion** controls barrel recoil and every other motion effect through the shared render presentation policy. Each presentation frame reads that policy, including ready, paused, Controls, results and zero-tick frames. Reduced motion or zero visual intensity clears active recoil, debris, dust, smoke, the ball trail, impact rings, camera kick and shake, the carriage squash, Gus's hops, fretting and idle bob, and the bell's swing; restoring motion cannot replay them, and events accepted while it is disabled cannot revive them. Score pops, banners and Gus's lines are information, so they remain, but they no longer rise, pop or slide, and the score total snaps instead of rolling. The independent reduced-flash setting clears and blocks the muzzle flash and impact flashes only. Shot/target text, target colors, audio, aiming, trajectories, scoring and timers remain unchanged.

Rust-red blocks count when they fall, rotate far enough, or move out of their original stack. The final cannonball gets six seconds of simulation time to finish its work before a loss; the HUD displays the settling countdown. A win or loss freezes the result and offers a fresh range. Pausing, losing focus or opening Controls suspends physics, reload and settling timers. Closing Controls or restoring focus resumes only if the player has not manually paused.

`ferravex_core::session::{GameSession, SessionState}` owns the host lifecycle and fixed clock. Physics and held-key/stick aim run at 120 Hz with at most twelve catch-up steps per frame; transforms interpolate between ticks, and cannonballs use continuous collision detection. Relative mouse/touch look is sampled once per presentation frame. Gameplay Fire uses `PlayerInput::take_tick` inside actual ticks, so quick press/release edges survive zero-step frames and cannot repeat across catch-up ticks. Visible Fire-button presses use a single bounded pending flag. Pause and rebuild discard stale edges. Terminal states stop the remainder of a catch-up batch. Rebuild resets both simulation resources and session clock. A bounded eight-projectile allocation avoids unbounded debris growth; native smoke flags and browser `?autoplay` remain supported.

Scoring is part of the fixed-tick simulation: knockdowns, wobbles, ball impacts and the bell are detected inside `Range::step` from real body state and reported as bounded per-tick events. Juice and Gus react only to those accepted events and age only with accepted ticks (the results-card reveal alone uses render time while the range is frozen), and effect randomness uses a fixed seed reset by Rebuild, so retries look identical. The bell is a sensor: it adds no collider and never changes a ball's path.

Regression tests exercise stable stacks, shot cooldown and ammunition exhaustion, CCD, the final-shot grace period, zero-tick/catch-up input through the real PlayerInput adapter, reload rejection, ready/pause/terminal states, rebuild resources, accepted feedback, recoil cancellation, remapped/unbound hints and bounded HUD metrics at 320/390/700/1280 pixels. Held aim produces the same result at 30/60/144 render frames per second. Autoplay runs through the same session host at 30 and 144 render frames per second and knocks down every target through normal ballistic contacts. It only aims and fires; it never moves targets or awards points directly. Further tests cover chain multipliers and bonus rules, miss declaration and late-topple retraction, stars and next-goal hooks, the bell aim through real physics (sensor only, not a miss), identical score tallies at 30/60/144 render frames per second, juice bounds/determinism/pause freezing, reduced-motion and reduced-flash clearing, camera kick preserving eye and depth, the reticle's target/support lookup, non-repeating quips, rank ordering, and HUD/card/bubble copy fitting at 320 to 1600 pixels. A compiling crate rustdoc example demonstrates session start, finish and restart. These native/headless checks do not establish fresh Wasm linkage, browser preference delivery, visual layout, physical controls or audible playback; parent integration owns those checks.

The range is a small single-player example. Blocks topple as rigid pieces rather than fracturing (debris chunks are cosmetic), and the arc preview stops at the first collision without predicting later deflections. Records are local to the device; there is no network mode. The restored renderer has Linux and WebAssembly hosts. Browser pointer/touch forwarding uses original platform bindings; physical touch devices and the updated HUD still need visual verification. Headless tests do not establish cross-platform physics determinism.
