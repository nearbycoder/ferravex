## Concrete behavior and compatibility

- Stores exactly two `T` values plus an `f32` alpha, hold flag and `u64`
  discontinuity generation. No collection, retained sampler/output, clock, input,
  tick identifiers, scheduling or simulation stepping. No special pose trait or
  competing transform/snapshot/ghost abstraction was introduced; a tuple or an
  existing pose type composes Vec3 and Quat through a supplied interpolator.
- `new` clones its initial value once, starts unheld at alpha 1, generation 0.
  `commit` moves current to previous, drops the oldest value, installs the new
  value without cloning, releases any hold and resets presentation alpha to 1.
  Only actual accepted updates belong in commits; zero-tick frames and skipped
  scheduled updates do not. Catch-up retains the last two executed states.
- `sample_with` accepts only finite alpha in inclusive `[0, 1]`, including either
  signed zero and exactly 1. Invalid input is checked even while held, returning
  `InvalidAlpha` without calling the sampler or changing any field. There is no
  clamping or extrapolation. On valid input the callback runs exactly once with
  borrowed previous/current endpoints and the effective alpha, even at endpoints
  or after snap. Its output type is unconstrained and not stored.
- Sampling records the effective fraction only after the callback returns.
  Endpoint contents and output validity are caller policy. A callback's own
  `Err` is an ordinary completed output (`Ok(Err(..))`) and still records alpha.
  A callback panic does not update pair metadata; side effects through captured
  state or interior-mutability in `T` remain caller-owned.
- `hold` idempotently retains the last successful fraction; subsequent valid
  samples ignore their supplied fraction. This prevents inactive `GameSession`
  alpha 0 from rewinding the existing pair. Reproducing the same image requires
  a pure interpolator and stable endpoints; no third cached pose is hidden.
  Before a sample after new/commit/snap, holding selects alpha 1/current.
- `resume` idempotently releases a hold, preserving endpoints, recorded alpha and
  generation. The next sample follows its supplied fraction. Normal zero-tick
  resume passes the active session's retained fraction. When an inspector showed
  alpha 1 during a pause, retaining the hold until the next ordinary commit avoids
  blending backward to the session's smaller fraction; this recipe is documented
  and behaviorally tested. There is no implicit monotonic-alpha clamp.
- A commit or snap always releases a hold. A presentation hold therefore cannot
  block an authoritative update. Freezing a separate picture while remote/replay
  state advances requires host-owned picture storage; two current tick samples
  cannot also retain a third independent frozen frame.
- `snap` is the reset operation for an accepted teleport, recovery, respawn or
  restart. It clones once, replaces both endpoints, clears hold, sets alpha 1,
  increments generation with wrapping, and returns that generation. Even snapping
  to an equal value advances it. Generations are local change markers, not issued
  or executed tick IDs or globally unique identifiers. New pairs start at 0,
  cloning copies it, and `u64::MAX` wraps to 0. Ordinary commits never advance it.
- Storage does not grow: a tracked non-Copy payload test performs 10,000 commits,
  checking two live retained values, no commit/sample/hold/resume cloning and full
  cleanup; new/snap each clone exactly once. Caller payload construction/Clone/Drop
  and samplers can allocate or panic. No unconditional allocation/panic guarantee
  is made for arbitrary `T`; the library itself uses safe Rust only.
